HE: <span class="mediumGrey">מנגנון השפיעה: שלמות פנימית ועודף שלמות</span><br><sup>4</sup> ודע שכל מי שמשפיע טוב כלשהו באופן הדירוג הזה – מציאותו של המיטיב הזה ומטרתו ותכליתו אינה להיטיב עם המוטב בלבד, אחרת נובע מכך אבסורד גמור. שהרי התכלית נכבדה יותר מן האמצעים לתכלית, והיה מתחייב (לפי זה) שמציאות הנעלה יותר והשלם יותר והנכבד יותר היא בשביל הנחוּת יותר. ואף בר דעת לא ידמיין זאת.
EN: It must be known that everything which, in this classification, communicats any good, has not for the sole final cause of its existence—though it be a <i>giver</i>[[fn:1556]]—the giving unto that which receivs; for (were it so), there would follow therefrom a manifest absurdity. Verily, the <i>end</i> is more noble than the things which exist for the sake of that end; now, it would follow (from the said supposition) that what is higher, more perfect, and more noble exists for the sake of that which is inferior to it, a thing which no intelligent man could imagine. But the matter is as I shall state: When a thing possesses a certain kind of perfection, sometimes this perfection occupies therein an extent (sufficient) for the thing itself to be perfect, without any perfection being communicated therefrom to another thing; sometimes the perfection has such an extent that there is a surplus wherewith to perfect another thing. Thus, to cite an example, thou wouldst say that there is a certain man who possesses a fortune sufficient only for his needs, and there remains naught in excess of which another may profit, and another who possesses a fortune of which there remains unto him a surplus[[fn:1557]] wherewith to enrich many people, so that he may give unto another person sufficiently for that person to be likewise rich and have enough remaining to enrich a third person. It is the same in the universe: the <i>efflux</i>, which comes from God to produce separate Intelligences, is also communicated from these intelligences so that they produce one another, even unto the Active Intellect, wherewith the production of <i>separate</i> (intelligences) ceass. From each separate (intelligence) there likewise emanats another production[[fn:1558]], until the spheres terminate at that of the moon. After this last comes this (lower) body which is generated and perishes, I mean prime matter and that which is composed thereof. From each sphere there come forces (which are communicated) unto the elements, until their <i>efflux</i> stays at the term of (the world of) generation and corruption.

HE: <sup>5</sup> אלא העניין כמו שאתאר: דבר השלם בשלמות כלשהי – יש ששלמות זו נמצאת בו במידה כזאת שהיא משלימה את עצמותו מבלי שתעבור ממנו שלמות לאחרים, ויש ששלמותו היא במידה כזאת שעודפת ממנו שלמות עבור אחרים. הרי זה כאילו אמרת, על דרך המשל, שיש אדם שיש לו ממון המספיק לצרכיו בלבד, בלי עודף שאחרים יוכלו ליהנות ממנו; ולאדם אחר יש ממון שעודף עליו ממנו מה שיוכל להעשיר אנשים רבים, עד שייתן לאדם אחר במידה כזאת שגם האדם הזה ייעשה עשיר, ועוד יישאר לו מה שיעשיר בו אדם שלישי.<br><sup>6</sup> כך הוא הדבר במציאות, שהשפע המגיע ממנו יתעלה להבאה למציאות של שכלים נבדלים, שפע גם מן השכלים ההם, להבאתם למציאות זה מזה, עד לשכל הפועל – ושם נעצרה הבאת הנבדלים למציאות. מכל נבדל שפעה גם הבאה למציאות עד שהסתיימו הגלגלים בגלגל הירח, ואחריו הגוף המתהווה וכלה שאצלנו, כלומר החומר הראשון ומה שהורכב ממנו. מכל גלגל מגיעים כוחות אל היסודות, עד שהשפע שלהם נגמר עם תום ההתהוות והכיליון.<br><span class="mediumGrey">הפילוסופיה היא המסורת הקדומה שלנו</span><br><sup>7</sup> כבר ביארנו (א,עא1; ב,ג1;ה1 ועוד) שכל העניינים האלה אינם סותרים דבר ממה שציינו נביאינו ומעבירי תורתנו, כי אומתנו אומה מלומדת ושלמה, כמו שביאר יתעלה על ידי האדון (=משה) שהביאנו לשלמות, ואמר: "רַק עַם חָכָם וְנָבוֹן הַגּוֹי הַגָּדוֹל הַזֶּה" (דברים ד,ו). אך כיוון שרשעי האומות הבורות כילו את הטוב שבנו, ואיבדו את דברי חכמתנו וחיבורינו, והרגו את חכמינו, עד שחזרנו להיות בורים, כמו שניבא עלינו רעות בשל חטאותינו ואמר: "וְאָבְדָה חָכְמַת חֲכָמָיו וּבִינַת נְבֹנָיו תִּסְתַּתָּר" (ישעיהו כט,יד), והתערבנו בהם – אימצנו את דעותיהם כמו שאימצנו את מידותיהם ומעשיהם. כמו שנאמר על חיקוי המעשים: "וַיִּתְעָרְבוּ בַגּוֹיִם וַיִּלְמְדוּ מַעֲשֵׂיהֶם" (תהילים קו,לה), כך נאמר על אימוץ דעות הבורים: "וּבְיַלְדֵי נָכְרִים יַשְׂפִּיקוּ" (ישעיהו ב,ו), ותרגם זאת יונתן ע"ה: "וּבְנִמּוֹסֵי עַמְמַיָּא יְהָכוֹן" (=ובחוקות הגויים ילכו). וכיוון שגדלנו על ההרגל לדעות הבורים, קיבלנו את העניינים הפילוסופיים האלה כאילו הם זרים לתורתנו כשם שהם זרים לדעות הבורים, ואין הדבר כן.
EN: We have already set forth that all these things do in no wise subvert the words of our prophets and the pillars of our Law[[fn:1559]]; for our nation was a learned and perfect nation, even as God has proclaimed by the hand of the Master who perfected us, saying: <i>Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people</i> (Deut. IV, 6). But when the wicked among the ignorant nations had brought to nought our fair qualities, destroyed our sciences[[fn:1560]] and our books, and put to the sword our wise men,—so that we became ignorant, even as it had been threatened us on account of our sins, saying: <i>The wisdom of their wise men shall perish</i>, <i>and the understanding of their prudent men shall be hid</i> (Isaiah, 29, 14),—we mingled with these nations[[fn:1561]], and their opinions passed unto us, as also did their manners and their actions. Even as it was said, concerning the assimilation of actions: <i>They were mingled among the heathen and learned their works</i> (Ps. 106, 35), so likewise was it said, concerning the opinions of the ignorant nations transmitted unto us: <i>And they please themselves in the children of strangers</i> (Isaiah, 2, 6)[[fn:1562]], which Jonathan ben-Uzziel translats: <i>And they follow the laws of the nations.</i> Since, therefore, we were nurtured in the habit of the opinions of the ignorant peoples, these philosophical matters appeared to be as foreign to our Law as they were to the opinions of the ignorant peoples, though in truth it be not so.

HE: <sup>2</sup> מה שהביא אותי לומר זאת הוא שהאחרונים ההולכים אחר אריסטו טוענים שאריסטו הוכיח את קדמות העולם, ורוב האנשים הסבורים שהם עוסקים בפילוסופיה מסתמכים על אריסטו בשאלה זו וחושבים שכל מה שאמר הוא הוכחה חותכת שאין בה ספק, וחושבים שמגונה לחלוק עליו או (לומר) שנעלם ממנו משהו או שהוא טעה בדבר כלשהו.
EN: — That which has induced me to speak of this is that the moderns among the followers of Aristotle claim that he has <i>demonstrated</i> the eternity of the world. Most of those who profess to be philosophers do therefore follow, in this question, the authority of Aristotle, believing that all which he has said is a decisive demonstration in which there is naught of doubt; and they even deem it absurd to gainsay him, or to suppose that anything could have remained hidden[[fn:1630]] from him, or that he could have erred in any matter whatsoever. Wherefore I have deemed it my duty to proceed with them according to their own opinion, and to show them that Aristotle himself does not pretend to give a demonstration upon this question. Thus, for example, he says in the <i>Acroasis</i>: “All the physicists who have preceded us believed that motion had no beginning and is imperishable, with the exception of Plato, who believed that motion had a beginning and is perishable; and likewise the heavens, according to him, had a beginning and are perishable.” Such are his expressions[[fn:1631]]. Now, it is clear that if this question had been demonstrated by rigorous demonstrations, Aristotle would have had no need to support it by the concordant opinion of the ancient physicists[[fn:1632]], nor would he have had need to say all that he has said in the same place to show the absurdity of those who gainsay him and to render their opinion contemptible; for, as soon as a thing is demonstrated, its truth cannot increase, nor can its certainty be strengthened by the common consent of all the learned, and on the other hand, its truth cannot diminish, nor its certainty be weakened, by the contradiction of all the inhabitants of the earth.

HE: <span class="mediumGrey">ראיות מאריסטו שאין לו הוכחה לקדמות העולם</span><br><sup>3</sup> לכן מצאתי לנכון ללכת איתם לשיטתם ולהבהיר להם שאריסטו עצמו לא טען להוכחה בשאלה זו, מדבריו ב"פיזיקה" (ח,1, 251נ): "כל אנשי מדעי הטבע שקדמו לנו מאמינים שהתנועה אינה מתהווה ולא כלה, מלבד אפלטון, המאמין שהתנועה מתהווה וכלה, וכן גם השמים לדעתו מתהווים וכלים". זה לשונו. ידוע שאילו היתה השאלה הזו מוכחת בהוכחות חותכות <br><sup>א)</sup> לא היה אריסטו נזקק לתמוך אותה בכך שאנשי מדעי הטבע שקדמו לו מאמינים כך, <br><sup>ב)</sup> ולא היה נזקק לומר כל מה שאמר באותו מקום, שגינה את מי שחולק עליו ואמר שדעתו פסולה; כי כל מה שהוכח – נכונותו לא תגדל והוודאות לגביו לא תתחזק בכך שכל העולם מסכים עליו, ונכונותו לא תחסר והוודאות לגביו לא תיחלש בכך שכל יושבי הארץ חולקים עליו.<br><sup>4</sup> תמצא גם את אריסטו בספר "השמים והעולם" (על השמים א,10, 279ב), כשהחל לבאר שהשמים אינם מתהווים ולא כלים, אומר כך: "אחר הדברים האלה, נרצה כעת לחקור גם על אודות השמים. ונאמר: האם נראה לך שהם התהוו מדָבר, או שלא התהוו מדבר? והאם הם בני כיליון, או שאינם כלים כלל?". לאחר שהציב שאלה זו ביקש להזכיר את טיעוניו של האומר שהשמים התהוו, ולאחר מכן אמר דברים בלשון זה: "בכך שנעשה זאת, יהיו דברינו מקובלים ורצויים יותר אצל המיטיבים לעיין, בפרט אם שמעו קודם את טיעוני החולקים. משום שאילו היינו אומרים את דעתנו ואת טיעונינו ולא היינו מזכירים את טיעוני החולקים, היה זה פחות נוח להתקבל אצל השומעים. וראוי למי שרוצה לדון לפי האמת שלא יהיה כאויב לחולק עליו, אלא יהיה נוח והוגן ויבחן את נכונות טיעוניו באותה צורה שבה הוא בוחן את נכונות טיעוני עצמו". זה לשון האיש.
EN: Thou shalt also find that Aristotle, in the treatise <i>On the Heavens and the World</i>, where he beginns to set forth that the heavens had no beginning and are imperishable, expresses himself thus: “We wish therefore, after this[[fn:1633]], further to make researches concerning the heavens, and we say: dost thou believe they were created from something, or that they were not? that they are subject to corruption, or that they shall never perish[[fn:1634]]?” After having posed this question, desiring (as he says) to report the arguments of those who say that the heavens had a beginning[[fn:1635]], he continus in the following terms: “When we shall have done this, our words will be received with more benevolence by those who excel in speculation; especially when they shall first have heard the arguments of the adversaries. For if, without reporting the arguments of our adversaries, we were to state only our own opinion and our arguments, these would appear to the hearers too weak to be accepted. It is worthy of him who wishes to judge with truth not to be hostile toward him who contradicts him; he ought, on the contrary, to be benevolent and impartial toward him, rendering justice to his arguments as to his own[[fn:1636]].” Such are the textual words of this man.

HE: הוא אמר שהחומר הראשון אינו מתהווה ולא כלה. והחל להביא לכך ראיות מן הדברים המתהווים והכלים, ולהבהיר שבלתי אפשרי שהוא התהווה. וזה נכון, כי אנחנו איננו טוענים שהחומר הראשון נוצר כמו היווצרות האדם מן הזרע, או כלה ככיליון האדם אל העפר. אלא אנו טוענים שהאל הביא אותו למציאות מן האין כשהוא במצבו לאחר שהובא למציאות, כוונתי לכך שממנו נוצר כל דבר וכל דבר שנוצר ממנו כלה אליו, ואין הוא קיים מופשט מצורה, וההתהוות והכיליון מגיעים בסופו של דבר אליו. התהוותו אינה כהיווצרות מה שנוצר ממנו, וכיליונו אינו ככיליון של מה שכלה אליו, אלא הוא מחודש, ואם המחדש שלו יחפוץ, הוא יגרום לו להעדר מוחלט וגמור.<br><span class="mediumGrey">דחיית הראיה הראשונה של אריסטו: נצחיות התנועה</span><br><sup>7</sup> וכך בדיוק אנו אומרים לגבי התנועה. כי הוא הביא ראיה מטבע התנועה, שאינה מתהווה ולא כלה; והדבר נכון, כי אנו טוענים שלאחר הימצאות התנועה בטבעה היציב הזה, אין לדמיין שהתהווּתה הכוללת וכיליונה הכולל הם כהתהוות התנועות החלקיות המתהוות וככיליון התנועות החלקיות, והוא הדין לכל מה שמחייב טבע התנועה.
EN: The First Matter, says he, has neither been born nor shall it perish; and, arguing from things born and perishable, he shows that it is impossible it should have been born[[fn:1664]]. And this is true[[fn:1665]]. For we do not maintain that the first matter was formed as man is formed from the semen, nor that it must perish as man perishes by becoming dust; but we maintain, on the contrary, that God produced it out of nothing, and that after its production it is such as it is[[fn:1666]],—I mean to say, that all things are formed therefrom, and that all which is formed therefrom returns thereto in perishing. It exists not divested of form[[fn:1667]], and it is the term of generation and of corruption. As for itself, it was not born (<i>of</i> anything), as everything is born which is formed from it, and it shall not perish (<i>into</i> anything), as that perishes which perishes within it; but on the contrary, it is a thing <i>created,</i> and whenever its Creator shall will it, He shall reduce it to pure and absolute non-existence.

HE: <span class="mediumGrey">דחיית הראיה השלישית של אריסטו: נצחיות הסיבוב</span><br><sup>8</sup> וכן האמירה שלתנועה הסיבובית אין התחלה היא נכונה לאחר ההבאה למציאות של הגוף הכדורי הנע סיבובית, שאין להעלות על הדעת התחלה בתנועתו.
EN: We shall say precisely the same thing concerning motion; for arguments have been drawn from the nature of motion to prove that it was not born and shall not perish[[fn:1668]]. And this is likewise true[[fn:1669]]; for we maintain that it is inconceivable that, since motion has existed with its invariable and fixed nature, it could, in its universality, be subject to birth and corruption, even as are the partial motions which are born and perish[[fn:1670]]. The same reasoning applies to all that is inherent in the nature of motion[[fn:1671]]. Likewise, when he says of circular motion that it has no beginning, this is true (in the sense) that after the <i>production</i> of the spherical body, which movs circularly, one could not imagine in its motion any beginning whatsoever[[fn:1672]].

HE: <span class="mediumGrey">ראיה מהשכל הפועל</span><br><sup>3</sup> הראיה לכך היא השכל הפועל לדעת אריסטו וההולכים אחריו, שהוא נבדל, והוא לעתים פועל ולעתים אינו פועל, כמו שביאר אבו נצר (אלפאראבי) בחיבורו "על השכל". הוא אמר שם דברים בלשון זה: "וברור שהשכל הפועל אינו פועל תמיד, אלא הוא פועל בזמן מסוים ואינו פועל בזמן אחר" (אלפאראבי, השכל והמושכלות, עמ' 108), זה לשונו, וזה אמת וברור. ולמרות זאת אין אומרים שהשכל הפועל משתנה ולא שהוא היה פועל בכוח ונעשה בפועַל משום שפעל בזמן מסוים מה שלא פעל לפני כן. כי אין יחס ואין שום דוֹמוּת בין הגופים לבין מה שאינו גוף, לא בעת הפעולה ולא בעת ההימנעות מפעולה. אלא פעולתן של הצורות בעלות החומר ופעולתו של הנבדל נקראות "פעולה" בשיתוף השם (=רב־משמעיות) בלבד. מסיבה זו, מכך שהנבדל אינו פועל בזמן מסוים את הפעולה שהוא פועל לאחר מכן, לא נובע שהוא יצא מן הכוח אל הפועל כמו בצורות בעלות החומר.
EN: Perchance it may be believed that in what I have just said there resids some sophistry: If, it will be said, the Active Intellect necessarily acts at one certain moment and acts not at another, this is not because of aught inherent in its essence, but because of the disposition of the materials; on its part, the action is perpetually exercised upon all that is <i>disposed</i>, and if there be aught that hinders the action, that proceeds from the <i>disposition</i> of the matter, and not from the Intellect in itself[[fn:1689]].— Let him therefore who thus thinks[[fn:1690]] well know that our purpose is not to make known the cause for which God acted at one certain moment and not at another; and, in citing this example, we have not concluded therefrom that, since the Active Intellect, which is <i>separate</i>, acts at one time and acts not at another, it must be the same with God[[fn:1691]]. We have not said that, nor have we made such a conclusion; and had we done so, it would indeed have been a sophistry. But what we have concluded therefrom—and it is a true conclusion—is that, although the Active Intellect, which is neither a body nor a force in a body, acts at one certain moment and performs not the same action at another moment, no matter what the cause thereof may be, it is not said on that account that it has passed from potentiality to actuality, nor that there has been in its essence any <i>possibility</i>, nor finally that it has need of an efficient cause to make it pass from potentiality to actuality[[fn:1692]].

Already Abu Nasr (Al-Farabi), in his glosses upon the <i>Acroasis</i>, has expressed himself in the following terms: «Between the sphere and the stars there is a difference; for the sphere is diaphanous, whilst the stars are not so. I, however, do not say <i>small</i>, but (I say) that they differ <i>greatly</i>; for I derive the proof thereof, not from the transparency, but from the motions. It is therefore clear to me that there are three matters and three forms: 1st, bodies which, in themselves, are always at rest, and these are the bodies of the stars; 2nd, bodies which are always in motion, and these are the bodies of the spheres; 3rd, bodies which sometimes move and sometimes are at rest, and these are the elements. In short, two diverse bodies, of which one is fixed in the other, without being mingled therein, and being, on the contrary, circumscribed in a particular place of the latter and strongly attached, (all this) without the design of a being acting with intention, would be an astonishing thing[[fn:1747]]. But, what is yet more astonishing, are those numerous stars which are found in the eighth (sphere), all of them globes, some small, others large, here a star, there another [apparently at the distance of a cubit], here ten (stars) clustered together, there a great space with nothing. What then is the cause which particularly distinguishes this space by ten stars and that other by the lack of stars? Finally, the body of the sphere is a single simple body, without diversity; for what cause, then, does such a part of the sphere suit the star located therein, rather than another? All this, like all things of the same kind, would be most improbable, or rather would border upon the impossible, were it admitted that all proceeds from God <i>by necessity</i>, as Aristotle supposs. But, as soon as it is admitted that all is due to the design of a Being acting with intention, who has fashioned it thus, there remains naught at which one need marvel[[fn:1748]], nor aught that is improbable; and there is no further room for enquiry, unless thou shouldst ask: what is the cause of this <i>design?</i>

EN: I shall explain to thee the sense of this last argument, after having first shown thee how little those who assail with their attacks the words of the prophets have understood these words (of David). Years ago, some physicians, men of wit of our own community, expressed to me their astonishment at these words of David. From his reasoning, said they, it would follow that He who created the mouth eats, that He who created the lungs cries out, and the same would hold for the other organs. But thou shalt see, O reader of this treatise, how far these persons were from understanding the import of this argument; harken to its meaning: It is clear that he who fashions any instrument whatever, if he possessed not the idea of the work which that instrument is to serve, would find it impossible to fashion an instrument for that use. If, for example, the smith did not form a just idea of sewing, he could not fashion the needle in a form which alone can make it serve for sewing, and the same is true of other instruments; for, as certain philosophers believed that God perceivs not individual things, which are things perceived by the senses, whilst God perceivs not by a sense, but by an intelligible perception, he (David) argus against them from the existence of the senses. If, says he, the manner in which the eye perceivs were for God a mystery which He were incapable of knowing, how could He have produced this organ, destined for visual perception? Was it pure chance that caused a limpid humor to be born, and then another similar humor, then a membrane which chance alone likewise would have perforated, and that finally before the opening there should be placed a transparent and hard membrane[[fn:2892]]? In short, can an intelligent man imagine that the humors, the membranes, and the nerves of the eye, which are so wisely organized[[fn:2893]] and whose whole has for its end this visual action, are a simple effect of chance? Assuredly not, and there is therein necessarily an <i>intention</i> of nature, as all physicians and all philosophers have declared. Now, nature has neither intelligence, nor (consequently) organizing faculty[[fn:2894]], whereupon the philosophers are agreed; but this artistic organization[[fn:2895]] emanats, according to the opinion of the philosophers, from an intellectual principle, and, according to us, it is the work of an intelligent Being who has impressed such faculties upon all that possesses a natural faculty. If, therefore, this Intelligence perceived not the object in question and knew it not, how, in that case, could it have produced or caused to emanate from itself a nature tending toward a goal which it knew not? It is therefore with reason that he (David) calls these men <i>brutish</i> and <i>fools.</i> Thereafter he setts forth that this is a defect of our perception. God (says he), who has given us this Intelligence by which we perceive, whilst our incapacity to grasp His true essence gives rise within us to these grave doubts, God knows this defect which exists in us, and we must not take account of the attacks which are the result of the weakness of our reflection[[fn:2896]]: <i>He</i>, says he, <i>that teaches man knowledge</i>, the <i>Lord, knows the thoughts of man, that they are vanity</i> (<i>ibid.</i>, <i>v</i>. 10-11).

EN: This principle they do constantly proclaim in all their discourses, and the texts of the Sacred Books do likewise indicate it. Nevertheless, I have found a passage of the Doctors in <i>Bereshith Rabba</i> which seems at first sight to say that certain commandments had no other reason than that of prescribing something[[fn:3086]], without any other end or real utility being in view. Thus it is there said: «What matters it to the Holy One whether one slays animals by the throat or by the nape of the neck? Thou mayest therefore infer thence that the commandments have no other purpose than that of refining men[[fn:3087]], as it is said: <i>The word of the Lord is pure</i> (Ps., 18, 31)[[fn:3088]].» Albeit these words be passing strange, the Doctors nowhere else pronouncing in a like sense, I have interpreted them, as thou shalt hear, in such wise as not to abandon the doctrine which they have constantly proclaimed[[fn:3089]], and not to depart from the principle upon which all are agreed, to wit, that all commandments have a truly useful end: <i>for it is not a vain thing</i> (Deut., XXXII, 47), and as it is said elsewhere: <i>I have not said unto the seed of Jacob: Seek ye Me in vain; I am the Lord, speaking righteousness, declaring things that are right</i> (Isaiah, 45, 19).

HE: <sup>7</sup> <sup>דוגמה 2)</sup> מה שבאמת ראוי להשתמש בו כדוגמה בעניין הפרטים, הוא הקרבן. כי לציווי בהקרבת הקרבן יש תועלת גדולה וברורה, כמו שאני עתיד לבאר (ג,מו). אך בשום אופן אי אפשר לתת טעם להיותו של קרבן מסוים כבש וקרבן אחר איל, והיות מספרם מספר מסוים. וכל מי שמעסיק את עצמו במתן טעם למשהו מן הפרטים האלה הוא לדעתי הוזה הזיה ממושכת שאין הוא מסיר על ידה אבסורד אלא מוסיף אבסורדים. מי שמדמיין שאפשר לתת להם טעם, רחוק מן האמת כמי שמדמיין שהמצוה בכללותה אינה לשם תועלת ממשית.
EN: That which every man of sound reason ought to believe in this regard is as follows: the general enactments of the commandments[[fn:3090]] have necessarily a reason and have been prescribed in view of a certain utility; but the <i>particular details</i>, it has been said, have no other end than to prescribe something[[fn:3091]]. Thus, for example, the precept of slaying animals for the need of good nourishment is of manifest utility, as we shall set forth[[fn:3092]]. But, if it be said that they must be slain by the upper part of the throat, and not by the lower[[fn:3093]], and that the gullet and the windpipe must be cut in a determined place, these and similar regulations have no other end than that of <i>refining men</i>[[fn:3094]]. This is what results from their manner of expression: «Whether one slays by the throat or by the nape.» Verily, I have only cited this example because we read in the passage of the Doctors these words: «Whether one slays by the throat or by the nape;» but if the matter be well examined, the truth is this[[fn:3095]]: As there is a necessity to feed upon the flesh of animals, the intention was to inflict the easiest death upon them and at once to obtain this in the simplest manner [for, to decapitate, a sword or other similar instrument would be required, whereas one may slay with any instrument whatsoever], and to bring about death more easily, it was made a condition that the knife be passing sharp. A more exact example of the <i>particular details</i> is found in the sacrifices. Indeed, the precept of offering sacrifices has a great and manifest utility, as I shall expound[[fn:3096]]. But, whether the victim be now a lamb, now a ram, and that the victims be of a fixed number, these are things for which no reason can ever be given. In my judgment, all those who take the trouble to seek reasons for any of these particular details betray a great folly; and so far from removing thereby any absurdity they might possess, they do but increase the absurdities. He who imagins that these details can be motivated is as far from the truth as he who believs that the general precept has no real utility.

But, when one continually approaches any object of respect, the effect which it produces upon the soul does diminish, and the impression received therefrom is lessened. The doctors have already called attention to this subject, saying that it is not well to enter at every moment into the sanctuary, and they cite in support these words: <i>Withdraw thy foot from thy neighbour's house; lest he be weary of thee, and so hate thee</i> (Prov., 25, 17)[[fn:3725]]. It was with this intention that God forbade the unclean to enter the sanctuary; and the cases of uncleanness being very numerous, one could but very rarely find a person who was pure. Indeed, even if one had preserved himself from the contact of a carcass, he might not have escaped contact with one of the <i>eight creeping things</i>[[fn:3726]] which often fall into the house, as well as into food and drink, and against which one often stumbls[[fn:3727]]. Having avoided that, one might still not have escaped contact with a woman in her courses or one afflicted with an issue of blood, a man afflicted with gonorrhœa, a leper, or their bed[[fn:3728]]; even had one escaped these, one could not always avoid cohabitation with his wife, or having a nocturnal accident[[fn:3729]]. Even when one had purified himself from these uncleannesses, it was not permitted to enter the temple before the setting of the sun[[fn:3730]]. EN: All this, therefore, contributed to the end that one should keep far from the sanctuary and not enter therein at every instant. Thou knowest, moreover, what the doctors say: “No person, were he even pure, shall enter the court for the service until he has bathed[[fn:3732]].” These acts, then, maintained reverence and served to produce the impression which was to lead to the piety that was the aim. In measure as the case of uncleanness might happen more frequently, the purification was more difficult and lasted longer. To be under the same roof with dead bodies, and especially with those of kinsmen and neighbours, is a case more frequent than any other uncleanness; one could not, therefore, become pure again except by means of the ashes of the <i>red heifer</i>, which are exceeding rare, and after seven days[[fn:3733]]. The flux (of blood or gonorrhœa) and the menses are more frequent than contact with an unclean thing; wherefore these uncleannesses required seven days (of purification)[[fn:3734]], and he who came into contact with them was unclean for a single day[[fn:3735]]. If the purification of the man afflicted with gonorrhœa, of the woman afflicted with an issue of blood, and of the woman in childbirth, is not completed but by a sacrifice, it is because these cases happen more rarely than the menses[[fn:3736]]. — All these uncleannesses, I mean those of the menses, gonorrhœa, issue of blood, leprosy, a dead body, a carcass, a creeping thing, and of semen, are things most foul. The legal dispositions relative thereto have, therefore, various ends: 1st, to remove from us all foulness; 2nd, to preserve the sanctuary; 3rd, to have regard for customs generally spread abroad, for thou shalt hear presently what grievous ceremonies the Sabians imposed upon themselves in these cases of uncleanness; 4th, to alleviate this heavy burden and to ensure that the question of what is pure or impure should not fetter man in any of his occupations, for this question concerns only the sanctuary and holy things: <i>She shall touch no hallowed thing, nor come into the sanctuary</i> (Levit., 12, 4). For the rest, one is guilty of no sin in remaining unclean as long as one wills, and in nourishing oneself as much as one wills with profane things tainted with uncleanness. According to the customs spread among the Sabians until our time in the countries of the Orient, I mean among the remnants of the Magi[[fn:3737]], the woman having her menses remains isolated in an apartment, the places upon which she walks are burnt[[fn:3738]], he who speaks to her becoms unclean, and if the wind that blows passes over the woman and over a pure man, the latter becoms unclean. Thou seest, consequently, how far these usages are from what we say: “All the works that a woman does for her husband, the woman having her menses may do likewise, except the washing of his face, etc.[[fn:3739]]”; one is only forbidden to cohabit with her during the days of her defilement and uncleanness. — Another custom spread among the Sabians until our time is that they repute as unclean all that is separated from the body, whether hair, or nail, or blood; wherefore every barber, among them, is unclean, because he touches the blood and the hair[[fn:3740]]. Whosoever has himself shaved must plunge into water springing from a fountain. They have many of these inconvenient usages, whilst we pay no attention to what is pure or impure save in regard to holy things and the sanctuary.

EN: However, if the Scripture says: <i>Ye shall therefore sanctify yourselves, and ye shall be holy; for I am holy</i> (Levit., 11, 44), this is by no means said in regard to what is pure or impure; the <i>Siphra</i> says expressly that it referrs therein to a “sanctification by the commandments[[fn:3741]]”, and likewise the doctors say, concerning these words: <i>Ye shall be holy</i> (Levit., 19, 2), that it referrs to a sanctification by the commandments. Wherefore the transgression of the commandments is also called <span dir="rtl">טומאה</span> (defilement or uncleanness), an expression employed in regard to the fundamental commandments[[fn:3742]], which are idolatry, incest, and murder. In speaking, for example, of idolatry, it is said: <i>because he has given of his seed unto Molech, to defile my sanctuary</i> (Levit., 20, 3); of incest, it is said: <i>defile not ye yourselves in any of these things</i> (<i>ibid.</i>, XVIII, 24); of murder, it is said: <i>so ye shall not pollute the land, etc.</i> (Numbers, 35, 34). It is seen, consequently, that the expression <span dir="rtl">טומאה</span> (defilement or uncleanness) is an homonymous term which is used in three different senses. It is said: 1st, of disobedience and the transgression of the commandments in matters of action or opinion; 2nd, of foulness and filth: <i>her filthiness is in her skirts</i> (Lamentations, I, 9); 3rd, of those things reputed (unclean), I mean when one touches or carries such a thing, or when one is found under the same roof with such a thing[[fn:3743]]. It is in this third sense that we say: “The words of the Law are not susceptible of uncleanness[[fn:3744]].” Likewise, the word <span dir="rtl">קדושה</span>, <i>holiness</i>, is said, as an homonym, in three senses, opposed to the three significations of which we have just spoken.

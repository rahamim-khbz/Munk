אין הַאֲמָנָה בלא הבנה
הגדרת ההאמנה
1 דע, אתה המעיין בחיבורי זה, שההַאֲמָנָה אינה העניין הנאמר, אלא העניין המצטייר (נתפס, מומשג) בנפש, אם משתכנעים לגביו שהוא כך כפי שהצטייר.
2 אם אתה מאלה שדי להם באשר לדעות הנכונות, או שאתה חושב שהן נכונות, באמירתן בפה מבלי לצייר אותן ולהאמין בהן, כל שכן לבקש להן ודאות – הרי דבר זה קל מאוד, כמו שאתה מוצא רבים מן הפתאים משננים אמונות מבלי שיציירו להן משמעות כלל.
Know447, O reader of this my present treatise, that belief is not a thing which is merely uttered, but a thing which is conceived in the soul, whilst it is believed that the object is even as it is conceived. If, therefore, in the matter of true opinions or those deemed to be such, thou art content to express them in words, without conceiving them or believing them, and, with even greater reason, without seeking therein a certainty, this is a thing right easy; and thus it is that thou findest many foolish men who retain in their memory beliefs of which they conceive absolutely no idea.
הימנעות מסיסמאות ריקות
3 אך אם אתה ממי שגמר אומר לעלות לדרגה הנעלה הזו, דרגת העיון, ולקנות ודאות שה' הוא אחד באחדות אמיתית, כך שאין לו שום מורכבות ואין לשער בו חלוקה בשום אופן שהוא – דע שאין לו יתעלה תואר עצמי בשום אופן ובשום מצב, ושכפי שמן הנמנע שהוא גוף כך מן הנמנע שהוא בעל תואר עצמי.
But if thou art of those whose thought does rise to ascend unto that exalted degree, which is the degree of speculation, and to possess the certainty that God is one, with a real unity, so that there be found in Him naught composite nor aught that is virtually divisible in any manner whatsoever, it behoovs thee to know that God has no essential attribute, under any condition, and that even as it cannot be admitted that He is a body, so is it inadmissible that He should possess an essential attribute448.
ואילו מי שמאמין שהוא אחד בעל כמה תארים – הוא אומר בפיו שהוא אחד, אבל מאמין במחשבתו שהוא רבים. הדבר דומה לדברי הנוצרים: "הוא אחד, אבל הוא שלושה, והשלושה הם אחד". כך הם דברי האומר: "הוא אחד, אבל הוא בעל תארים רבים, והוא ותאריו אחד, יחד עם סילוק הגשמות והאמנת הפשטות הגמורה", כאילו מטרתנו וחקירתנו אינה אלא איך נאמר ולא איך נאמין.
ציור, האמנה וודאות
4 ואין האמנה אלא לאחר ציור (בדעת), כי ההאמנה היא השכנוע במה שהצטייר, שהוא מחוץ לדעת (=במציאות האובייקטיבית) כפי שהצטייר בדעת (=במחשבה הסובייקטיבית). אם משיגים עם האמנה זו שלא ייתכן כלל דבר שונה מאותה האמנה, ולא ייוותר בדעת מקום לדחיית אותה האמנה ואין לשער אפשרות דבר שונה ממנה – זוהי ודאות.
Whosoever should believe that He is one, yet possessing manifold attributes, would indeed declare by his speech that He is one, but in his thought he would believe Him to be manifold. This would resemble that which the Christians say: «He is one, yet He is three, and the three are one»; for it would be the same thing if one said: «He is one, but He possesses many attributes, and He with His attributes are one», whilst setting aside corporeality and believing in the absolute simplicity (of God), as though our purpose were merely to seek how we should express ourselves, and not what we ought to believe449. There can be no belief except where there has been conception; for belief consists in admitting as true that which has been conceived, and in believing that the thing exists outside the mind even as it has been conceived in the mind. If there be joined unto this belief the conviction that the contrary of that which is believed is absolutely impossible, and that there exists in the mind no means of refuting this belief, nor of thinking that the contrary might be possible, that is certainty.
5 כאשר תפשוט מעליך את התאוות וההרגלים, ותהיה בעל תבונה ותתבונן במה שאומר בפרקים הבאים על שלילת התארים, יהיה לך הדבר ודאי בהכרח, ותהיה אז ממי שמצייר (בדעתו) את ייחוד השם, לא ממי שאומר זאת בפיו ואינו מצייר לו משמעות, שהוא מאלה שנאמר עליהם "קָרוֹב אַתָּה בְּפִיהֶם וְרָחוֹק מִכִּלְיוֹתֵיהֶם" (ירמיהו יב,ב). אלא ראוי שיהיה האדם מאלה המציירים את האמת ומשיגים אותה, אף על פי שאינם מוציאים אותה בפיהם, כמו שנצטוו אנשי המעלה ונאמר להם: "אִמְרוּ בִלְבַבְכֶם עַל מִשְׁכַּבְכֶם וְדֹמּוּ סֶלָה" (תהילים ד,ה).
If thou divestest thyself of desires and habits, if thou art intelligent and ponderest well what I shall say in these following chapters concerning the negation of attributes450, thou shalt necessarily have certainty in this regard, and then shalt thou be of those who conceive the unity of God, and not of those who merely pronounce it with their mouth, without conceiving an idea thereof, and who belong unto that class of whom it has been said: Thou art near in their mouth, and far from their reins (Jeremiah, xii. 2). For it behoovs man to be of those who conceive the truth and understand it, even though they should not utter it, as it has been commanded unto virtuous men, saying unto them: Commune (think) with your own heart upon your bed, and be still (Ps. iv. 4).

Note 447: This chapter serves as an introduction to what the author will set forth, in the ten ensuing chapters (LI to LX), concerning the [[t:0]]attributes[[t:1]]. Ere he addresses his wholly spiritualistic theory of the [[t:2]]attributes of God[[t:3]], a theory so far removed from vulgar beliefs, he deems it incumbent upon himself to offer a definition of [[t:4]]belief[[t:5]], that is to say, of such belief as truly merits this appellation, and which is not a mere [[t:6]]profession of faith[[t:7]], but an intimate [[t:8]]thought[[t:9]], an [[t:10]]idea[[t:11]] which, even though it may not be objectively true, is at least so in the mind of him who has conceived it, and contains nothing absolutely inadmissible.
Note 448: The question of [[t:0]]attributes[[t:1]] is one of those which most occupied the Muslim and Jewish doctors of the Middle Ages. Certain doctors, whilst professing the absolute unity of God, yet believed themselves able to admit a certain number of eternal and [[t:2]]essential[[t:3]] attributes, that is to say, attributes inherent in the divine essence, such as life, knowledge, power, etc.; the sect of the [[t:4]]Mo’tazales[[t:5]], in general, denied [[t:6]]attributes[[t:7]] as incompatible with absolute unity; there were, however, among them, those who implicitly admitted [[t:8]]essential[[t:9]] attributes by asserting that God is living through His essence, and not through the [[t:10]]attribute[[t:11]] of life; that He knows through His essence, and not through the [[t:12]]attribute[[t:13]] of knowledge, and so forth. (Cf. Pococke, Spec. hist. Arab., p. 214 et seq., and [[t:14]]ibidem[[t:15]], the text of Abou’l-Faradj, p. 19). These latter, according to our author, are no more in the right than those who openly profess the attributes of God. Faithful to the principles of the philosophers, he rejects [[t:16]]essential[[t:17]] attributes in an absolute manner. See further details below, in chap. LIII.
Note 449: Those, says the author, who assign to God numerous attributes whilst proclaiming His unity, His incorporeality, and His absolute simplicity, are in contradiction with themselves, and it would appear that, according to them, the matter concerns rather a particular mode of expression than to penetrate themselves with the true sense of beliefs. The [[t:0]]Mo’tazales[[t:1]], in arguing against the proponents of eternal and essential attributes, reproach them, as does our author, with falling into an error similar to that of the Christians, who admit three persons in God. Cf. Pococke, [[t:2]]loco citato[[t:3]], p. 216: [[t:4]][Lat.: Christians are accused of infidelity because they establish three eternal things; what then is to be pronounced concerning those who establish seven or more?][[t:5]]
Note 450: That is, on the necessity of excluding from God all affirmative attributes.
